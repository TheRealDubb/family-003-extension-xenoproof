# The quasi-Riemann hypothesis

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis: A Zero-Free Half-Plane $\Re(s)>7/8$](../../preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/paper.pdf)
- [Uniform exclusion of Landau–Siegel zeros](../../preprints/Uniform-exclusion-of-Landau-Siegel-zeros-October-1-2026/paper.pdf)

## Scope

The quasi-Riemann hypothesis asks for a fixed zero-free half-plane $\Re s>\theta$ with $\theta<1$. The original formalization gives $\theta=7/8$ for the Riemann zeta function and every Dirichlet $L$-function, uniformly over all positive moduli and all characters. It also establishes the same bound for finite-order Hecke $L$-functions over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$.

The principal-character poles at $s=1$ are excluded in the Dirichlet and Hecke statements. The paper's later applications are not included.

The formalized result gives a uniform logarithmic exclusion region for Landau–Siegel zeros. There is one constant $c>0$ such that every primitive nonprincipal real Dirichlet character of conductor $q\ge3$ and every real zero $0<\beta<1$ of its $L$-function satisfy $1-\beta\ge c/\log q$.

Both character parities are included. No explicit value of $c$ is given. This excludes real zeros in $1-c/\log q<\beta<1$, but does not rule out real zeros elsewhere in $(0,1)$.

## Stronger rational boundary

The additional public theorems strengthen the previous $\Re(s)>7/8$
boundary to the strict half-plane

$$\Re(s)>\frac{8749570194201}{10000000000000}=0.8749570194201<0.875=7/8.$$

They use the same `riemannZeta`, `DirichletCharacter.LFunction`, and
`OAI.SevenEighths.HeckeFamily.LFunction` definitions and pole conventions.
The Hecke statement quantifies over the original finite-order
`HeckeFamily.Character` family over the Eisenstein field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$;
it is not restricted to characters of order three. The zeta statement retains
Mathlib's value at `s = 1`; the Dirichlet and Hecke statements exclude their
specified principal-character poles. No claim is made on the boundary line.

The entrypoint is
[`OAI.BelowSevenEighthsTight.AssemblyExtension.PublicBoundary`](../OAI/BelowSevenEighthsTight/AssemblyExtension/PublicBoundary.lean).
In namespace `BelowSevenEighthsTight.PublicResults`, its three nonvanishing
theorems are:

- `riemannZeta_ne_zero_of_rational_lt_re`
- `dirichlet_LFunction_ne_zero_of_rational_lt_re`
- `hecke_LFunction_ne_zero_of_rational_lt_re`

Their hypotheses contain no additional moment, capacity, probe, contour, or
analytic-contract assumptions. The extension retunes rational parameters and
their exact certificates through the analytic proof; it introduces no stronger
analytic input. The original statements above remain available unchanged.

the pinned dependencies and official compatibility patches provisioned, run
from `lean/`:

```sh
lake build OAI.BelowSevenEighthsTight.AssemblyExtension.PublicBoundary ComparatorChallenges.FrontierTightPublicBoundary
```

For dependency provisioning, follow [the repository's patch instructions](../patches/README.md).

and [comparator configuration](../ComparatorChallenges/FrontierTightPublicBoundary.json)
cover all three results and the exact strict boundary comparisons. Its
intentional statement holes are not solution proofs. Compile/export fixture
and solution separately; they intentionally declare the same theorem names.

`Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. Verification used the native Windows
development route; the Linux Landrun/Landlock production sandbox has not been
